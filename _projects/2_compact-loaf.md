This page is a public note and a private crib sheet: the machine, the bags on the shelf, and the one water measure that stopped being an argument.

The point of the formula is a good loaf with a modest potassium load. Store bread left the house more than a year ago. This one is simply better to eat. Four hundred grams is the right size for one or two people: there are no preservatives, so the loaf does not keep for long, which is why two or three bakes a week is the rhythm.

###### **The machine**

Panasonic **SD-PN100KXE** — the small 2022 European model. Official flour load: **250 g**. Finished loaf: about **400 g**, roughly 13 × 13 × 12 cm.

Program: **Basic**, about four hours. Rapid saves time and costs volume and flavor.

This pan loads the opposite way from many other machines. The manual is right:

1. Dry yeast in the bottom
2. Flours
3. Salt, sugar, fat
4. Water last

Yeast: **Lesaffre Saf-Instant**. After the 500 g pack is open, keep a few days’ worth in small airtight boxes in the fridge; freeze the rest.

Water: **170 ml**. That number held across T55, Caputo, and rice-flour blends. Use lukewarm water only when the kitchen is cold. Never hot.

A little **olive oil** keeps the crust from turning into a lid. With no fat, the crumb stays wetter than it should.

###### **Why two flours**

This is not a gluten-free loaf. Wheat does the work. A fifth of the bowl is white rice flour because it softens the crumb and keeps the formula on refined, lower-mineral flours.

|                                  | Weight | Role                                                               |
| -------------------------------- | -----: | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Caputo **Manitoba Oro** (Type 0) |  200 g | Structure and spring in a short machine cycle                      |
| White **rice flour**             |   50 g | Softer crumb; a modestly lower mineral load than an all-wheat loaf |

Cornstarch (maize starch) was a dead end: the loaf rose and then sank. It does not come back.

Portuguese supermarket rice flours, coarsest to finest in this bowl: **Espiga** → **Ceifeira** → **Cem Por Cento**. The bag worth hunting is Caputo **Nuvola di Riso** (or Sarchio _impalpabile_) when it is in stock. Finer rice flour absorbs more cleanly and dries the crust a little.

###### **Caputo wheat: three bags, three jobs**

Italian “00” and “0” are ash grades — how thoroughly the bran is milled out — not strength. Strength is protein, and the mill’s **W** number.

**W is a dough-strength index** from the Chopin alveograph: a standard mill test that blows a thin sheet of dough until it bursts. A higher W means the dough can hold gas for longer. It has nothing to do with electrical power.

| Flour                            |   Protein | Strength                | Machine loaf?      | What you get                                                      |
| -------------------------------- | --------: | ----------------------- | ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Manitoba Oro** (Type 0)        | ~14–14.5% | High (W 360–390)        | Yes — first choice | Tall loaf, elastic crumb; holds up with 20% rice flour            |
| **Cuoco** (Chef’s, Type 00)      |      ~13% | Medium-high (W 300–320) | Yes                | A little shorter than Manitoba; still a good everyday loaf        |
| **Pizzeria** (Blue bag, Type 00) |    ~12.5% | Medium (W 260–280)      | Usable, not ideal  | Tighter crumb, less height. Milled for pizza, not a four-hour pan |

**Saccorosso** sits nearer Cuoco than Manitoba: usable, not the first choice here.

When the Manitoba bag is empty, buy Manitoba again. Switching to the blue pizza bag to shave a few milligrams of minerals is not worth the shorter loaf.

###### **Storage**

Caputo ships in 5 kg paper. Here in the Ribatejo, a hot inland region of Portugal, a paper bag in the pantry is not enough in summer. Keep the working few kilos in an airtight bin; clip the rest of the bag shut in the coolest cupboard.

The loaf lives in a **bread tin**, not a linen bag. Linen keeps the crust proud and dries the crumb. After a season of crusts that were too hard, a soft crumb and a soft crust in a tin is the point.

###### **What this is not**

It is not pizza dough and it is not a bakery schedule. It is one small loaf, the same water every time, and two flours with jobs that do not overlap.
